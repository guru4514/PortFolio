01 / THE CONTEXT

A problem worth
working through.

The problem

A single number cannot explain why one resume appears above another. A useful screening workflow needs to show which requirements match, which are missing, and how the comparison was formed.

The approach

TalentRank parses resume documents, extracts skills, and scores candidates against a job context. The result includes score components, matched and missing skills, and related-skill evidence for review.

02 / HOW IT WORKS

From a document to inspectable evidence.

Follow the path through the application.

STEP 01 / 04

Provide context

The job description, role family, and required skills establish the comparison criteria.

STEP 02 / 04

Parse documents

The parser extracts resume text and profile signals; the preview endpoint exposes extracted content before analysis.

STEP 03 / 04

Match & score

Exact and related skills are compared with requirements. Role-specific weights produce component scores and a total.

STEP 04 / 04

Compare evidence

The interface presents rankings alongside the evidence needed to inspect and question them.

03 / THE DECISIONS

The choices
inside the code.

01

Make the score explain itself

The scorer combines required skills, must-have skills, nice-to-have skills, experience, and role fit. It returns those components alongside the total instead of exposing only a final ranking.

THE TRADEOFF

The weights encode assumptions. They need evaluation against a defined comparison set; a high matching score is not proof of hiring suitability.

02

Separate exact and related evidence

The skill taxonomy supports exact matches and related-skill matches. The scorer gives those different contributions and handles missing must-have skills explicitly.

THE TRADEOFF

Related skills can recover useful overlap, but should remain distinguishable from direct evidence of a requirement.

03

Treat malformed documents as an expected input

The repository includes parser and endpoint tests for corrupted PDF and DOCX uploads. Parsing failures are converted to controlled responses rather than unhandled exceptions.

THE TRADEOFF

Document parsing can still fail or lose context. A reviewable preview gives users an opportunity to notice extraction errors.

06 / SCOPE & NEXT STEPS

Good engineering
leaves room to improve.

The score measures heuristic alignment with specified requirements. Its value is in the component evidence: a reviewer can inspect a match, question a weight, or spot an extraction error before making a decision.

WHAT WOULD MAKE IT STRONGER

A documented ranking benchmark, explicit evaluation criteria, and analysis of false matches would make the quality of the ranking easier to assess.
